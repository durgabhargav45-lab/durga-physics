What to notice (answers Activity 12.1 and 12.4)
  • Key closed: the needle deflects. Current produces a magnetic effect.
  • Reverse the battery: the needle deflects the opposite way. The field direction depends on current direction.
  • More current: more deflection. Move the compass away: less deflection.
  • Why not 90°? The Earth's own field also pulls the needle north. The needle settles where the two pulls balance: tan(angle) = wire field ÷ Earth's field.

Drag the compass near the north pole. Its south end turns towards it: unlike poles attract.
Rules of field lines. They leave the N pole, enter the S pole, and continue inside from S to N, so they are closed loops. Crowded lines mean a strong field. They never cross.
Why can two field lines never cross?

At a crossing point the compass would have to point in two directions at once. A needle can only point one way, so the lines cannot cross.

Solenoid: inside the coil the lines are parallel and evenly spaced, so the field is uniform. Outside it looks just like a bar magnet. Field at centre = n times one turn.
Quick trick to find the north end

Curl the fingers of your right hand the way the current goes around the coil. Your thumb points to the north end of the solenoid.

Electrons, protons, alpha particles (Example 12.2 and exercise questions)

Current direction means the direction positive charges move. A proton or alpha particle moving east is a current going east. An electron moving east is a current going west. So for electrons, point the middle finger opposite to the motion. Try it in the challenge.

8. Electric motor

Bonus lab · the tough one made easy

A motor is just Fleming's left-hand rule used twice, once on each side of a coil, with a clever switch that keeps the push going the same way.

Three questions, three answers. Why does the coil turn? The two sides get opposite pushes. Why does it not stop when it becomes vertical? Its own momentum carries it. Why must the current flip? Otherwise the pushes would reverse and drag it back.
Drag to rotate the 3D view. Use Freeze and the angle slider to step through one turn.
End view: you look along the axle. Green arrows are the forces from Fleming's left-hand rule.

How a motor works in 4 steps
  1. Current flows down side AB and back along side CD, so the two sides carry opposite currents.
  2. Fleming's left-hand rule gives one side an upward push and the other a downward push. That pair of pushes is a turning effect.
  3. At the vertical position the forces still act, but they only stretch the coil. No turning. The gap in the split ring reaches the brushes and the current reverses.
  4. The reversed current flips both pushes, so the coil keeps turning the same way through every half turn.

Try Slip rings and see the coil stall and wobble. That is exactly why the split ring is needed.
